# Build the Verified AI Journey Diagnostic Incumbents Won't

The Big-4 and MBB firms do not possess a secret methodology for the three-part engagement you describe; what they possess is a benchmark database, a hypothesis-driven storyline discipline, and partner-level review, and all three can be replicated by an AI-first firm provided it solves the one problem the incumbents have publicly failed at: verifying that every cited fact exists and says what is claimed. The public evidence base is now rich enough to design each step. Maturity assessment converges on 5–8 dimensions and 4–5 levels, with the leader share stable at roughly **5–8% of companies** across BCG, Accenture, MIT CISR, McKinsey and IDC, so any instrument that puts more than 15% of clients in the top band is miscalibrated ([BCG](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-Sept-2025.pdf), [IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/ambition-is-everywhere-maturity-is-rare-inside-idcs-2026-ai-maturityscape-benchmark/)). The "why they exist" step has a merged six-part test drawn from Strategy&, Helmer, Barney and Porter, plus a 2026 AI-era overlay in which each strength is tagged strengthened, eroding or hollowing ([BCG, Aug 2026](https://web-assets.bcg.com/pdf-src/prod-live/which-companies-capture-value-from-ai.pdf)). Value-pool identification reduces to an ROIC driver tree feeding a concentration rule of 2–5 domains, with core functions holding **62–75% of AI value** ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/choose-the-right-transformation-bite-size), [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value)). Use-case selection is a value-by-feasibility scorecard with hard gates, positioned inside domains rather than as a catalogue. The evidence quality behind all of this is mostly correlational and self-reported, which is itself a design input: separate self-reported capability from independently evidenced outcome, and publish the rubric. On the AI-research side, independent audits show deep-research agents hallucinate 3–13% of URLs and that only 24–77% of citations actually support the attributed fact, with accuracy falling ~42% as tool calls rise ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1), [arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635v1)); Deloitte's A$440K refund in Australia and C$1.6M Newfoundland report show what happens when that risk reaches a client. Commercially, **88% of clients bought AI consulting** in the past year yet **70% say they would not trust an AI-produced report** ([Source Global](https://sourceglobalresearch.com/blog-post/understanding-the-market-for-ai-related-consulting-services), [ResultSense/Telegraph](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-04-consultancy-clients-distrust-ai-reports/)), so the defensible position for a Singapore-based firm is "AI-accelerated, human-accountable, source-verified", sold as a fixed-fee 2–4 week assessment in the S$15K–85K band (vs. a ~S$400K Big-4 anchor) with the fee credited toward outcome-priced implementation.

## Maturity models agree on dimensions but hide their rubrics

Every serious framework published between 2024 and 2026 scores a near-identical set of dimensions: strategy and ambition, data, technology platform, talent, operating model and workflow redesign, governance and responsible AI, and value realisation. BCG's 2025 instrument rates **41 capabilities across strategy, technology, people, innovation and outcomes**, weights them to a 0–100 score and bins them as Stagnating (0–25), Emerging (>25–50), Scaling (>50–75) and Future-built (>75–100); the 2025 distribution was 5%, 35%, 46% and 14% respectively, with weights validated by random forest, logistic regression and PLS-SEM against CapitalIQ and Orbis financials ([BCG PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-Sept-2025.pdf)). PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study collapses **60 practice areas into nine factors** across "AI foundations" and "AI use", and defines leaders as the top 20% by AI-attributable financial performance, who capture 74% of returns ([PwC NL](https://www.pwc.nl/en/insights-and-publications/themes/digitalization/want-returns-from-ai-accelerate-your-growth.html)). Accenture's 2025 clusters (Experimenting 42%, Progressing 43%, Fast-followers 7%, Front-runners 8%) come from hierarchical clustering with Gower's distance on five capabilities plus five gen-AI essentials ([Accenture PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-3/Accenture-Front-Runners-Guide-Scaling-AI-2025-POV.pdf)). Gartner uses a seven-question 1–5 instrument on which high-maturity organisations average 4.2–4.5 and low-maturity 1.6–2.2 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-30-gartner-survey-finds-forty-five-percent-of-organizations-with-high-artificial-intelligence-maturity-keep-artificial-intelligence-projects-operational-for-at-least-three-years)), and IDC's 2026 MaturityScape scores four dimensions (Strategy, Governance, People, Technology) on a 1–5 scale with a global mean of **2.43** and only 3.1% at the Optimized stage ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/ambition-is-everywhere-maturity-is-rare-inside-idcs-2026-ai-maturityscape-benchmark/)). The most important 2026 design shift is that IDC split governance out as its own scored dimension, and the agentic era has added items on agent governance, human-in-the-loop design and share of decisions automated across BCG, McKinsey, Deloitte and PwC.

The single most consistent and observable level threshold across all models is the **production boundary**: Gartner Level 3, IDC's Opportunistic-to-Repeatable transition, MIT CISR's Stage 2-to-3 move (where the largest financial jump occurs), and score 3-to-4 on the academic 10-K rubric all mark the point where AI leaves pilots and enters production with an owner, budget and value tracking ([MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/whats-your-companys-ai-maturity-level), [arXiv 2607.08920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08920)). The top level is marked everywhere by AI embedded in decisions and sold in products. Observable discriminators of leaders are also stable: BCG's future-built deploy 62% of initiatives versus 12% for laggards and are 3x as likely to have a Chief AI Officer; Gartner's high-maturity firms keep 45% of projects in production for three-plus years versus 20% and 91% have a dedicated AI leader; McKinsey's high performers (defined as >5% of EBIT from AI, ~6% of respondents) are 2.8x as likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows ([McKinsey State of AI 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/the-state-of-ai-in-2025-vf.pdf)).

The evidence quality is weaker than the confident headlines suggest, and this matters for how you present scores. Every leader-versus-laggard multiple (1.7x revenue growth, 1.6x EBIT margin, 3.6x TSR in BCG; +7pp revenue growth and +4pp ROIC in Accenture) is correlational, and BCG, Accenture, Yu et al. and Babina et al. all say so explicitly. BCG also warns its data is self-reported and most impact figures are expected rather than realised ([BCG PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-Sept-2025.pdf)). IDC found that of self-identified AI "thrivers", only about **1 in 6 scored Managed or Optimized** on its own instrument, which is the cleanest demonstration of perception bias in the field ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/ambition-is-everywhere-maturity-is-rare-inside-idcs-2026-ai-maturityscape-benchmark/)). The Federal Reserve showed that a single wording change in the Census BTOS question raised reported AI adoption by 47–159% depending on industry ([Fed FEDS Note](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)). A 2021 systematic review of 15 AI maturity models found only 7 validated, technology over-weighted at 46% of attention, and several models that never explain how levels are scored ([PeerJ/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8409328/)). No firm publishes its full question bank or weights.

This creates the opening. The outside-in signals that have peer-reviewed or central-bank validation are exactly the ones an AI-first firm can automate: AI-paragraph scoring of annual reports on a 1–5 rubric correlates **0.76 with Census adoption data and 0.87 with Ramp spend data** at sector level ([arXiv 2607.08920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08920)); AI-skill share of job postings and resumes (Babina et al.) predicts sales, employment and market value growth ([NBER](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f160998/f160998.slides.pdf)); earnings-call AI semantic density predicts Tobin's Q and capex ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/research-in-commotion-measuring-ai-research-and-development-through-conference-call-transcripts.htm)); and BCG's future-built hold 3.5x the patents of laggards. Two caveats bound the claim: firm-level precision of these composites is unproven, and narrative signals are inflated by "AI-washing" post-ChatGPT, so hiring and patent evidence should outweigh what the CEO says on the call. None of the three academic strands detects productivity gains, which is a useful corrective when a client expects the maturity score to translate directly into margin.

| Design choice | Best public practice | Recommendation for the AI-first instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensions | 5–9 (BCG 5 strategies/41 items; PwC 9 factors; IDC 4) | 8: Strategy & ambition; Value realisation; Data; Platform & LLMOps/agent infra; Talent; Operating model & workflow redesign; Governance/RAI (ISO 42001 / NIST AI RMF mapped); Culture & adoption |
| Items and levels | 41–60 items × 4–5 behaviourally anchored levels | 48 items × 5 levels, anchors published to the client |
| Score and bands | 0–100, four named bands | 0–100 with four bands calibrated so top band ≈ 5–10% of benchmark |
| Two axes | BCG "value gap" separates capability from outcome | Report capability maturity and value maturity separately; never merge |
| Evidence classes | Self-report dominates; IDC re-scores | Every item scored from three evidence classes: self-report, interview, outside-in signal; show the variance |
| Benchmark | PwC 500+ organisations, Bain/WGS peer scorecard | Seed a proprietary benchmark with a free self-scored instrument; disclose n per peer set |

## "Why they exist" needs a six-part test and an AI-era tag

Strategy firms do not derive a company's distinctive capabilities from inspiration; they run candidates through a filter. Strategy&'s coherence framework is the only canonical text that puts a number on it: a company's right to win rests on a **system of three to six capabilities** that reinforce one another, a way to play, and product-service fit, with the test posed as "Can we state it?" and "Do we live it?" ([Leinwand & Mainardi, HBR 2010](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/gx/en/insights/2002-2013/coherence-premium/hbr-coherence-premium.pdf)). Helmer's 7 Powers reduces any claim to two necessary and sufficient conditions, a material benefit and a barrier that holds against every motivated competitor ([Acquired transcript](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/acquired/7-powers-with-hamilton-helmer)). Barney's VRIO adds the organisation test, and Porter's activity-system fit explains why systems of activities are more sustainable than individual ones because imitators must copy the whole chain ([VRIO](https://strategicmanagementinsight.com/tools/vrio/), [Porter 1996](https://maaw.info/ArticleSummaries/ArtSumPorter96.htm)). Merged, these produce a six-criterion scorecard for each candidate strength: material customer benefit; rare versus named competitors; barrier to imitation; organised and lived; system fit with the other strengths and across the portfolio; and breadth of market access. A strength passing only the first three is a temporary edge. A list of more than six passing strengths is itself evidence of incoherence, and the empirical reason to care is that only **20% of executives agree on which capabilities are key** in Strategy&'s 6,000-leader profiler, with 38% of companies classified "adrift" ([Strategy&](https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/gx/en/unique-solutions/cds/the-strategy-crisis.pdf)).

The data that evidences distinctiveness is consistent across Bain, McKinsey and Strategy&, and some of it comes with hard thresholds. McKinsey's power-curve work defines "differentiation" as **gross margin in the top 30% of the industry** ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/strategy-to-beat-the-odds)); Bain's Sustainable Value Creators earned at least 5.5% real growth in both revenue and earnings over ten years while covering cost of equity, and relative market position explains roughly **80% of the variation in financial returns** in Bain's 8,000-company database ([Bain Repeatability](https://www.bain.com/contentassets/71b8e7f706094d0eba442518366c527c/repeatability-excerpt---chapter-one.pdf)); Strategy&'s coherence score correlates with 2003–07 EBIT margin and capability-fit M&A deals earned ~12 percentage points higher two-year TSR CAGR ([s+b](https://www.strategy-business.com/article/12105)). The practical evidence pack per strength is therefore relative share or #1/#2 position, gross margin or price realisation versus peer median, ROIC above WACC over five-plus years, retention and NPS in core customers, win/loss and churn reasons that name the capability unprompted, and a competence-to-offering matrix. These studies are all firm-produced and correlational; the deliverable should say so.

The method for surfacing strengths from customers is published by specialist firms rather than MBB. Clozd's win/loss architecture starts broad and open-ended, covers product, sales experience, pricing, competitive landscape and buying committee, and bans rating scales in interviews ([Clozd](https://www.clozd.com/blog/a-framework-for-creating-the-right-win-loss-analysis-questions)); the JTBD switch interview reconstructs a 30–90-day decision timeline so the four forces emerge from events rather than opinions ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/switch-interview/)). A workable protocol is 8–12 external interviews across recent wins, losses and churn plus 6–10 executive interviews using the "state it / live it" questions, with a strength counted as evidenced only when customers name it in the decision phase, executives name it in their 3–6 list, and the economics show it.

Two things have changed since 2010. First, the LLM can now mine review corpora, earnings-call Q&A and analyst notes at scale, but only within known reliability limits: GPT-4 reaches human-level agreement (κ ≥ 0.70) on 25 of 34 constructs when the construct is concrete and the codebook is deductive, and drops to κ 0.10–0.12 on interpretive ones ([JLA 2025](https://www.learning-analytics.info/index.php/JLA/article/download/8575/7877)); inductive thematic analysis produced quotes consistent with themes only 33–87% of the time ([Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-18969-w)); and on earnings-call analysis models fabricated a specific answer **48.3% of the time** when supporting evidence was removed ([arXiv 2610.00969](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00969v1)). "Reason for choosing this vendor" is a concrete construct, so extraction reliability should be at the high end, but every quote and number must be human-verified and the model must be instructed and tested to abstain. BCG's own survey of strategy leaders found AI has delivered consistent gains only in market intelligence and research, not in judgment-heavy work ([BCG, Mar 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/the-corporate-strategy-function-in-an-ai-first-world)), which is a fair description of where automation belongs in this step.

Second, AI changes which strengths count. Barney and Reeves argue gen AI cannot itself be a sustainable advantage because rivals get the same access ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/ai-wont-give-you-a-new-sustainable-advantage)); BCG's August 2026 work says advantages that persist are physical assets, **flow data** continuously generated by the product, network scale and trusted relationships, while knowledge, stock data and operational excellence erode and can "look intact internally while weakening externally" ([BCG PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/pdf-src/prod-live/which-companies-capture-value-from-ai.pdf)). McKinsey's April 2026 piece adds that productivity gains are competed away to customers and durable moats are proprietary data that improves with use, AI embedded in workflows, faster learning cycles and ecosystem breadth ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/where-ai-will-create-value-and-where-it-wont)). Each of the 3–5 reasons the client exists should therefore carry an AI-era tag (strengthened, eroding, hollowing) and pass BCG's "hollowing check": margin and pricing power versus headline share and retention. This single overlay is the biggest substantive difference between a 2026 deliverable and a Strategy& document from 2010, and it links step one directly to step two, because an eroding strength is a value pool.

## Value pools come from a driver tree and a 2–5 domain rule

The top firms locate value in two layers. First, a top-down ROIC or TSR driver tree decomposes operating profitability and capital efficiency into revenue, cost and capital-intensity drivers with operational KPI anchors such as OEE, purchase price variance, cost-to-serve and churn ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/selecting-p-and-l-linked-kpis-for-industrial-transformations)); Deloitte's Enterprise Value Map and Kaplan-Norton strategy maps serve the same function. Second, a bottom-up pipeline of sized initiatives is concentrated into a few "domains". McKinsey defines a domain as a cohesive set of related activities (a customer journey, process or unit), says a monoline business has 10–15, recommends starting with **2–5**, and reports that up to 80% of successful interventions in struggling digital transformations re-anchored scope on a well-defined domain ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/choose-the-right-transformation-bite-size)). BCG tells CEOs to select 3–5 functions or processes across Deploy (10–15% productivity), Reshape (30–50% efficiency) and Invent, target 2–3x ROI, and allocate effort 10% algorithms, 20% technology and data, **70% people and process** ([BCG CEO's Guide 2024](https://www.bcg.com/assets/2024/executive-perspectives-ceos-guide-to-maximizing-value-from-ai-3july.pdf)); its 2025 AI Radar found leaders prioritise about 3.5 use cases versus 6.1 for others ([BCG AI Radar 2025](https://web-assets.bcg.com/0b/f6/c2880f9f4472955538567a5bcb6a/ai-radar-2025-slideshow-jan-2025-r.pdf)). Private-equity value-creation plans keep to 8–10 levers, each "named, sized, sequenced, owned, tracked" on an EBITDA bridge ([VCP guide](https://www.notveryprivateequity.com/what-is-a-value-creation-plan-in-private-equity/)). McKinsey's transformation data adds a sizing lesson: successful transformations deliver 2.7x their initial estimate, more than half the value comes from top-line initiatives, and about **55% comes from many small initiatives** each under 0.5% of full potential, so the pipeline behind the 3–5 headline domains must be oversized and long-tailed ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.de/capabilities/transformation/our-insights/how-bold-is-your-business-transformation-a-new-way-to-measure-progress)).

Where the value sits is unusually well triangulated. MGI put 75% of gen-AI value in customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering and R&D ([MGI 2023](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/reports/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)); BCG's leaders draw 62% of AI value from core functions and 70% of potential sits there, with IT at 13% ([BCG Oct 2024](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value), [BCG Oct 2025](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-October-2025.pdf)). The default hypothesis for any client is therefore two core domains plus one support domain. By industry, MGI's intensity table (high tech 4.8–9.3% of revenue, banking 2.8–4.7%, pharma 2.6–4.5%, retail 1.2–1.9%) combined with BCG's function shares (customer service is 24% of AI value in insurance, R&D 29% in automotive, personalisation 22% in retail) gives a first-domain heuristic per sector ([MGI PDF](https://www.mckinsey.de/~/media/mckinsey/locations/europe%20and%20middle%20east/deutschland/news/presse/2023/2023-06-14%20mgi%20genai%20report%2023/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier-vf.pdf)). These are 2023-vintage ceilings, which is the central calibration issue. Realised results are far smaller: in McKinsey's 2026 survey the share reporting any revenue increase tops out at 37% in marketing and sales, most reported cost decreases are **10% or less**, only 37% attribute any EBIT impact to AI and the 6% high-performer share has been flat for two years ([McKinsey State of AI 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/the%20state%20of%20ai/the-state-of-ai-in-2026-on-the-road-to-roi.pdf)). Goldman's chief economist said AI contributed "basically zero" to US GDP growth in 2025 ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ai-contributed-basically-zero-to-the-us-economy-last-year-according-to-goldman-sachs)). A credible value study presents both bands, technical potential and realised-to-date, per domain.

The three value lenses have different evidence grades. For "make money", the function-level revenue signals are weakest and most self-reported. For "save money", the best-evidenced numbers are customer support (+14% issues resolved per hour, +34% for novices, in a 5,179-agent RCT) ([NBER w31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)), back-office BPO and agency elimination ($2–10M a year, 30% of external creative spend per MIT NANDA), and procurement (2–3% on commodities to 10–15% on indirects) ([Activant](https://www.activantcapital.com/pdfs/ai-in-procurement.pdf)). Software engineering is the most volatile: a 56% speed-up on a toy task versus METR's **19% slow-down** for experienced open-source developers in 2025, revised to a confidence interval spanning zero in 2026 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), [METR update](https://evals.alignment.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)), so a 0–15% net band for seniors with in-engagement measurement is the honest default. Customer service also carries the highest reputational risk, as Klarna's reversal after claiming its assistant replaced 700 agents shows ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/klarna-backtracks-ai-customer-service-plans)). For "avoid risk", the most citable anchors are IBM's $4.44M average breach cost with **$1.9M saved** where security AI is extensively used ([IBM via Help Net Security](https://helpnetsecurity.com/2025/08/04/ibm-cost-data-breach-report-2025)), MGI's $180–260B for risk and legal, and the fact that only 5% of firms have the full control set needed for agents with decision authority while 42% expect to grant it by 2030 ([BCG, Sep 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2026-ai-starting-to-pay-off-companies-generate-value)). Agent governance is therefore a built-in "avoid risk" workstream in almost every engagement.

The ranking of the client against competitors should rest on the only outside-in method with published statistical backing: McKinsey's economic-profit power curve, where NOPLAT minus a capital charge ranks ~2,400 firms, the top quintile captures nearly 90% of economic profit, industry explains about half of position, and ten endowment, trend and move variables predict mobility ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/strategy-to-beat-the-odds)). Around it sit KPI decomposition with percentile ranks, a move-threshold scorecard (capex/sales top 20%, gross margin top 30%, M&A cadence, resource reallocation) and external proxies such as NPS Prism, app ratings and workforce signals. Peer selection must be documented: seed from SSIC/NAICS/GICS and named competitors in annual reports, score text similarity of business descriptions (the Hoberg-Phillips TNIC approach better predicts who managers themselves name as rivals than fixed codes) ([NBER w15991](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15991)), filter to 0.3x–3x revenue and the same regulatory regime, keep 5–12 core peers with a labelled secondary tier, and include exited or failed peers to avoid survivorship bias ([peer-group guide](https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/valuation-investment-banking/selecting-the-peer-group-criteria-that-matter)). For APAC private mid-market clients, Thailand's DBD and Singapore's ACRA Corporate Compliance and Financial Profile (S$50) are rare sources of cheap private-company financials; Korea's DART and Japan's EDINET are the richest free structured sources; India and China private financials are the hardest to obtain legitimately, so explicit gap disclosure plus listed regional proxies is the credible move ([companydata.com](https://companydata.com/company-register/singapore/), [Global Database](https://www.globaldatabase.com/full-directory-of-official-company-registries-and-data-sources-in-asia-pacific)).

## Use cases are scored inside domains with hard gates

No public library delivers industry by function by value lens by technique in one place. Google Cloud's catalogue is the largest free source at **1,302 entries** organised by industry and six agent archetypes (customer, employee, creative, code, data, security) ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/gen-ai-business-use-cases/)); Deloitte's AI Dossier (135+ cases by industry, function and AI type, with six value lenses) is the closest to a consulting-grade taxonomy ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/generative-ai-dossier)); Gartner's Use-Case Prisms and Assessments score value against seven feasibility dimensions into "Likely Wins, Calculated Risks, Marginal Gains" on an 18-month horizon but are paywalled ([Gartner via Freshworks](https://www.freshworks.com/freshservice/resources/gartner-ai-use-case-report/)). The right design composes them: a capability map (BIAN for banking, or a generic business capability map) answers where, the Deloitte value lens answers why, the agent-archetype and technique ladder answers how, and Gartner-style tiering answers when. McKinsey has explicitly abandoned use-case catalogues for domains, arguing that use-case-by-use-case efforts "failed to scale and never showed up on the P&L" and that fewer than 10% of vertical use cases get past pilot ([McKinsey WIRED](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/wired-the-ai-playbook-top-companies-use), [McKinsey agentic](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/seizing-the-agentic-ai-advantage)). An AI use case recommended for a named outcome must therefore be positioned inside one of the 3–5 domains from step two, and the technique ladder matters because value scales with it: McKinsey's illustrative call-centre ladder runs from 5–10% for assistants, through 20–40% for agents bolted onto old workflows, to up to 80% of incidents resolved autonomously when the process is redesigned around agents.

Scoring is a weighted value-by-feasibility matrix with pass-fail gates, and the only published weights come from practitioners rather than the Big 4. The ranges converge: business value 35–45%, feasibility 25–35%, time-to-value 10–15%, reuse 10–15%, strategic fit 5–10%, with risk and adoption effort as penalties, and data rights, decision ownership, baseline, measurement plan and safety boundary as hard stops ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/supply-chain-frameworks/ai-use-case-prioritization-framework/), [Okhrem](https://paul-okhrem.com/ai-use-case-prioritization-framework/)). DBS, the strongest APAC reference, measures every use case in three buckets, revenue, cost savings and risk avoidance, against control groups, which is exactly the make-money, save-money, avoid-risk structure you want, and reports **~S$1bn of economic value in 2025** from 430+ use cases ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/dbs-banks-billion-dollar-ai-dream-realized)). Singtel runs ~50 initiatives across 8 value pools targeting 5–10% of FY27 EBIT ([Singtel SGX filing](https://links.sgx.com/1.0.0/corporate-announcements/423V7E007WOXU5ET/902534_Investor%20Day%202026%20-%20Singtel%20SG.pdf)). Commonwealth Bank's halving of scam losses and 30% fraud reduction, Macquarie's 40% cut in false positives, OCBC's AML model and Toyota's ~$800M supply-chain digital twin give you APAC-anchored exemplars for each lens ([CFOtech AU](https://www.cfotech.com.au/story/commonwealth-bank-uses-ai-to-enhance-customer-service), [McKinsey WIRED](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/wired-the-ai-playbook-top-companies-use)). Prefer investor-day and annual-report figures over vendor blog metrics, which are self-reported pilot numbers.

The business case for each selected use case has a settled shape even though no Big 4 firm publishes a filled template: a system-measured baseline, three scenarios with the conservative case at 80% of benefits and 20% longer implementation, three-year TCO including run costs and tokens, payback expectations of 12–18 months for efficiency tools, 24–36 for quality and 36–60 for transformation, a do-nothing case, an accountable owner with stop conditions, and a control-group measurement plan ([AI Assembly Lines](https://aiassemblylines.com/post/ai-business-case-template-cfo-approval-guide)). Gartner prices business-model-transforming gen-AI initiatives at **US$5–20M** and McKinsey warns recurring costs can exceed build costs at scale ([IntelligentCIO/Gartner](https://www.intelligentcio.com/apac/2024/08/05/gartner-predicts-30-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025/)). Deloitte's data says 70% of firms expect at least 12 months to resolve ROI challenges, so only narrow efficiency tools should promise sub-12-month payback ([Deloitte Q4 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/about/2025/quarter-4.pdf)). Every use case also needs a responsible-AI tier: EU AI Act risk class, NIST AI RMF function mapping and ISO/IEC 42001 control mapping, which one assessment can serve ([Elevate Consult](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/ai-governance-frameworks-compared-matching-nist-eu-ai-act-and-iso-42001-to-your-use-case/)).

Failure evidence should be quoted with its definition because the headline rates measure different things: Gartner's 30% PoC abandonment and >40% agentic cancellation by 2027 are project-level; S&P Global's 42% of businesses scrapping most initiatives is firm-level; MIT NANDA's "95%" is the share of organisations without a P&L-visible production tool and has been widely criticised for its denominator ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ai-project-fail-data-spglobal/742590/), [claim check](https://www.ai-first.company/evidence/claim-checks/mit-95-percent-of-ai-pilots-fail/)). The recipe for success is consistent across six independent sources: one accountable senior owner, workflow redesign rather than tool insertion, a measured baseline and control group, data and integration foundations, 70% of effort on people and process, buy or partner where a vendor exists (vendor partnerships reach deployment about twice as often as internal builds in MIT's sample), and fewer, larger bets in core processes.

## AI deep research fails on citations unless you gate it

The automation case is real but bounded. Deep-research agents are now standard inputs and AlphaSense, Hebbia, Rogo and Fintool are selling vertical versions into finance at roughly $3,300–10,000 per seat ([Marvin Labs](https://www.marvin-labs.com/blog/ai-tools-for-equity-research-complete-platform-comparison/)). Independent audits, however, describe a specific failure profile. Across systems **3–13% of cited URLs are hallucinated** and 5–18% do not resolve, with deep-research agents hallucinating more than plain search (10.7% versus 4.8% pooled); an automated URL-health self-correction loop cut non-resolving rates below 1% ([arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1)). Link validity is not the hard part: frontier models keep links valid over 94% of the time, but the share of citations that actually support the attributed fact ranges from 24% to 77%, and fact-check accuracy fell about 42% on average as tool calls rose from 2 to 150, so more retrieval produces less accurate citations ([arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635v1)). About 16% of sources cited by generative search engines are themselves AI-generated ([AIES 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/05/25/preprint-synthetic-sources-auditing-generative-search-engine-citations-for-evidence-of-ai-generated-sources/)), and the Tow Center found chatbots answered over 60% of news-sourcing queries incorrectly and rarely declined ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). The consulting incidents map precisely onto this profile: Deloitte Australia's A$440K DEWR report contained fabricated academic references and an invented court quote, was traced to a GPT-4o toolchain, and ended in a partial refund ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/07/deloitte-ai-australia-government-report-hallucinations-technology-290000-refund)); Deloitte Canada's C$1.6M Newfoundland health workforce plan carried at least four citations to non-existent papers ([HR Reporter](https://www.hrreporter.com/focus-areas/automation-ai/ai-errors-province-grapples-with-deloitte-report-marred-by-false-citations/393782)). The incumbents' published controls are input controls (no client data in public LLMs, RAG over curated IP, slide-style graders like BCG's Deckster); none describe a mandatory output-citation verification step, which is the exact gap.

Two further findings from controlled studies on consultants shape the workflow. In the BCG/HBS RCT of 758 consultants, GPT-4 users inside the "frontier" completed 12.2% more tasks 25.1% faster at ~40% higher quality, but outside it were **19 percentage points less likely** to reach the correct recommendation ([Lakhani](https://professorkl.substack.com/p/discovering-ais-jagged-frontier-and)). Follow-up work found that when consultants pushed back, the model escalated persuasion rather than disclosing limits, and about 10% of consultants became "self-automators" who developed neither domain nor AI skill. The design response is to have the human form an independent hypothesis and ghost deck before AI synthesis, and to flag every AI-generated recommendation by confidence and source class.

Procurement is converting this from craft into contract. Australia's APS AI Plan requires suppliers on management-advisory panels to disclose planned AI use in RFQ responses and holds consultants fully responsible for output regardless of gen-AI use; Canada's PSPC now requires advance AI disclosure ([Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9110516/new-ai-plan-for-the-aps-sets-out-expectations-for-contractors/), [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2025/12/18/feds-defend-11-million-deloitte-contract-for-ai-advice-after-firm-admitted-mistakes/)). In Singapore, IMDA's AI Verify Foundation is explicitly building a third-party assurance market with mandatory external testers ([AI Verify Foundation](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/2025-main-report/introduction/)), and the new EDGE grant requires an SS 680-certified consultant for ISO/IEC 42001 projects ([Twenty Eight Eleven](https://twentyeighteleven.sg/blog/edge-grant-ai-projects-singapore)). "Big 4 quality" for the firm's own process therefore means an ISQM-style engagement file (acceptance, scope, consultation, review evidence, root-cause log), ISO 20700 contracting-execution-closure artefacts, the MBB storyline canon (ghost deck first, action titles under 15 words, horizontal-flow test, source line on every data slide, independent partner review) ([Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/consulting-quality-slides)), and an AI-use disclosure statement in every deliverable.

## A proposed end-to-end methodology for the firm

The following design integrates the evidence above into an operating model. It is organised as five phases, each with inputs, automation, QA gates and outputs, followed by the scorecards, evidence standards and deliverable list.

**Phase 0, Scoping and acceptance (days 1–2).** Confirm client identity, segments and the three value lenses in scope; run client acceptance and conflict checks; agree the peer-set protocol and the data the client will supply (P&L by segment, KPI extracts, win/loss data, customer lists for interviews). Output: engagement charter with the storyline hypothesis tree, ghost deck of action titles, and the AI-use disclosure.

**Phase 1, Outside-in research sprint (days 2–8).** Automated agents build the corpus across nine modules: business model; market size and trends; competitive landscape and peer benchmarking; customer evidence (reviews, NPS proxies, concentration); operations and talent signals (job postings, leadership churn, role ratios); technology and product (technographics, patents); financial and KPI decomposition versus peers; risks (regulatory, ESG, key-person); and value-creation plan credibility. Each metric carries a data-confidence label: filed, vendor-estimate, model-inferred, or interview-anecdote. The peer set is built by the documented funnel (seed codes and named competitors, TNIC-style text similarity, 0.3x–3x size filter, 5–12 core plus secondary tier, exited peers included). Outside-in maturity signals are computed from annual-report AI scoring on the 1–5 rubric, AI-skill share of postings, earnings-call density, leadership structure, patents and disclosed AI spend. QA gate 1 is automated: every URL health-checked, every claim fact-checked against its cited page, agent runs capped at bounded tool-call depth and chunked by module, aggregator-only sources traced to the original study, and AI-generated or content-farm pages rejected. The cost floor for a credible data stack is well under US$10K a year (ACRA, exchange portals, Crunchbase Pro, Statista Starter, BuiltWith, metered scraping), rising to US$50–150K for AlphaSense, PitchBook, Similarweb and a labour-data licence ([marketintelligencetools.com](https://marketintelligencetools.com/rankings/alternative-data-providers/)).

**Phase 2, Client inputs and primary research (days 5–12).** A structured self-assessment on the 48-item instrument goes to 10–30 leaders (structured assessments scale at near-constant review effort); 6–10 executive interviews use the "state it / live it" and three-circles questions; 8–12 external win/loss, switch and churn interviews run on the timeline protocol; KPI extracts populate the driver tree. QA gate 2: leadership-perception variance computed and shown; every interview quote verified against the recording or transcript; anecdotes separated from repeated patterns before anything affects a score.

**Phase 3, Synthesis (days 10–16).** The maturity score is produced on two axes, capability (0–100, four bands) and value (EBIT attribution, deployment share, tracking rigour), each shown against the peer benchmark and a global benchmark with n disclosed. The 3–5 reasons the client exists are produced by running every candidate through the six-criterion test, the evidence pack, and the AI-era tag. The 3–5 value pools are produced by the driver tree, a 10–15 domain scan, the value-by-feasibility screen, and the two-core-plus-one-support default, each sized with a technical-potential band and a realised-to-date band. Use cases are generated inside each domain from the composed catalogue, scored on the weighted matrix with hard gates, laddered by technique, and given a business case and RAI tier. QA gate 3 is human: an independent reviewer who did not run the agents performs the horizontal-flow test, the so-what test, and a line-by-line verification of every number and quotation in the exec summary; the lead partner signs each finding; the engagement file records who accepted what.

**Phase 4, Delivery and hand-off (days 16–20).** Board-ready storyline deck, the full evidence appendix, a 30/60/90-day plan plus 6- and 12-month roadmap, and the measurement design (baselines, control groups, owners, stop conditions) that becomes the basis of outcome-priced follow-on work. The benchmark database is updated with the anonymised scores.

The scorecards sit at three points.

| Scorecard | Structure | Calibration anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Maturity (capability axis) | 8 dimensions × 6 items × 5 levels, weighted to 0–100, four bands; governance items drawn from ISO 42001 Annex A and NIST AI RMF | Top band ≈ 5–10% of benchmark; production boundary at the band 2/3 line |
| Maturity (value axis) | EBIT attribution band (none / <5% / ≥5%), share of initiatives in production, value-tracking rigour, agent control maturity | McKinsey 6% high-performer cut; BCG 62% vs 12% deployment |
| Right-to-win test | 6 criteria scored 0–2 with evidence class per cell; AI-era tag; hollowing check | Pass all six = right-to-win; pass 1–3 = temporary edge; >6 passing = incoherence flag |
| Domain screen | Value (CX, financial KPI on tree, speed to value 6–36 months, synergy) × feasibility (sponsorship, data/tech, adoption, scaling) | 2–5 domains; two core + one support default |
| Use-case matrix | Value 40 (revenue/cost/risk-avoided, size × confidence), feasibility 25, time-to-value 10, reuse 10, strategic fit 10; risk −5 to −10 and adoption −10 as penalties; gates on data rights, owner, baseline, measurement plan, safety boundary | 3–5 use cases per domain; realisation discount applied to MGI-style ceilings |

The evidence standard defines five source tiers: T1 regulator filings, primary documents and peer-reviewed or RCT evidence; T2 company investor materials, central-bank and official statistics; T3 named consulting-firm research and reputable trade press; T4 vendor and practitioner content, clearly labelled; T5 aggregators and SEO content, used only for discovery. Every load-bearing number needs two independent upstreams (not two aggregators of one study), a source line resolvable to a T1–T3 document, and a correlational-versus-causal label. Figures from vendor case studies are presented as directional. Gaps are disclosed rather than estimated silently, which is what the ICAEW guideline requires of commercial due diligence ([ICAEW CDD Guideline](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ie/pdf/services/deal-advisory/ie-commercial-due-diligence-guideline.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)).

The deliverable list, triangulated from PwC, Bain/WGS, Deloitte and the boutiques and extended for your three outputs, is: executive storyline deck; two-axis maturity scorecard with peer percentile and leadership-perception variance; competitive ranking exhibit on economic profit and KPI percentiles with the documented peer-set protocol; the 3–5 reasons the client exists, each with evidence pack and AI-era tag; the 3–5 value pools with driver-tree sizing in two bands; 9–15 use cases with business cases, technique ladder position and RAI tier; responsible-AI and agent-governance assessment mapped to ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF; 30/60/90-day plan and 6/12-month roadmap; measurement design; evidence appendix with source log and confidence labels; AI-use disclosure and verification statement; and the engagement file.

The business model follows from the market data. Demand is near-universal and trust in AI-produced reports is low, so the product is sold on verification rigour, speed and disclosure, never on "AI-generated". Big 4 day rates of £1,050–2,855 imply a three-person, four-week assessment lists at roughly £250–350K, or S$400K-plus, which is the anchor to price against ([ConsultingDemand](https://consultingdemand.com/blog/consultant-day-rates-2024/)); UK public awards for AI readiness or strategy cluster at £27–104K and Singapore assessments run S$15–35K for firms under 100 staff, S$40–85K for 100–1,000 and S$90–200K above that, with 6–12 week strategy work at S$50–280K ([Pertama Partners](https://www.pertamapartners.com/insights/ai-consulting-pricing-guide)). A three-tier ladder matches observed behaviour: a free self-scored benchmark as lead magnet that also builds the proprietary benchmark set that PwC advertises as its moat (500+ organisations, percentile ranking); a fixed-fee, two-to-four-week, source-verified AI journey assessment at S$25–85K depending on client size, fee credited against follow-on within 90 days; and outcome-priced implementation at 60–70% base plus 30–40% performance bonus or 15–25% of savings, which is where McKinsey (about a third of revenue underwriting outcomes, target over half) and BCG (~75% of largest AI cases on variable fees) are heading ([HBR IdeaCast](https://hbr.org/podcast/2026/01/where-mckinsey-and-consulting-go-from-here), [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/consultants-put-skin-in-the-ai-game-as-clients-demand-proof-of-results/)). Because clients increasingly expect AI efficiency to arrive as a discount, the margin must come from the high-fixed, low-marginal cost structure: the research stack, the benchmark database and the verification pipeline are built once and amortised, and a retainer product (S$5–12K a month for ongoing diagnostics and benchmark refresh) converts the assessment into recurring revenue. Singapore-specific differentiators the incumbents are not packaging for SMEs are EDGE-grant navigation (up to 50% co-funding for SMEs under a S$100K annual cap, likely requiring an SS 680-certified lead for strategy work), AI Verify and Moonshot-aligned testing inside the assessment, and ISO/IEC 42001 readiness; the Australian redistribution of federal spend from the Big 4 (down A$800M) to mid-tier firms (up four-fold to A$1.7B) is the regional tailwind ([Canberra Times](https://www.canberratimes.com.au/story/9061287/rise-of-mid-tier-consultancies-amid-big-four-contract-cuts/)).

## Conclusion

The incumbents' moat in this specific engagement is thinner than their brands suggest. Their maturity rubrics are unpublished and self-reported, their use-case catalogues have been abandoned in favour of domain thinking that any firm can adopt, their value-sizing ceilings are 2023-vintage numbers that realised results undercut by an order of magnitude, and their internal AI controls stop at the input side, which is why their two public failures were fabricated citations. What they do have is a benchmark database, a hypothesis-first storyline discipline and a partner who signs. An AI-first firm reaches Big-4 level by building those three things deliberately and then exceeding the incumbents on the fourth, auditable output verification, at a point when Australian and Canadian procurement has made that verification a contractual obligation and Singapore is constructing an assurance market around it.

The deeper implication is that the three outputs you specified are one analysis, not three. A distinctive capability that is "hollowing" under AI is a value pool; a value pool that sits in a core function is where 62–75% of AI value lives; and a use case that does not sit inside such a domain, with an owner, a baseline and a control group, is one of the 30–95% that fail depending on whose denominator you use. Designing the methodology so the AI-era tag on each strength feeds the domain screen, and the domain screen feeds the use-case gates, is what turns a readiness report into a document a board can underwrite, and it is also what makes the outcome-priced tier, where the real margin now sits, something the firm can safely sign.
