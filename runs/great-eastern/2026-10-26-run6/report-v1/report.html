The annual report names eight uses of AI, from an advisory platform for financial advisers to automated medical claims and compliance monitoring. AI for complex underwriting cases is still being tested.2

2

Reported outcomes are speed, not yet dollars

In 2025, 75% of medical claims were settled same day, and the advisory platform targets ~75% less preparation time for financial advisers. 0 of 8 AI uses are reported with a dollar benefit, and neither figure is given with a baseline.3

Two named directors are paid for their data and AI work, and the Data & AI operating model was restructured in 2025 under a Managing Director, Group Data and AI. AI policy, an AI inventory and model risk management for AI are not covered in the annual report.5

Great Eastern discloses how its profit would change if each of its main actuarial assumptions moved on its own. In scenario 4, accident and health claims are 10% lower than assumed. On the 2025 figures, one year’s profit after tax would rise by S$31.8 million in Singapore and S$99.3 million in Malaysia, net of reinsurance: S$131.1 million in total. (Annual Report 2025, Note 30, pp. 186 and 187)

How to read this

This is Great Eastern’s own assumption sensitivity, not a forecast of AI benefit. It shows the scale of profit that moves with accident and health claims experience, which is where claims AI acts. Contractual life benefits, such as maturities, death benefits and surrenders, are excluded: they are obligations to policyholders, not costs for AI to reduce.

What the annual report describes

AI reaches the board in two places: two named directors are to receive fees for their contributions to IT infrastructure, data and AI initiatives, and the board’s collective competencies include artificial intelligence. (Annual Report 2025, pp. 71 and 78)

AI also has dedicated leadership. The Data & AI operating model was restructured in 2025, and a Managing Director, Group Data and Artificial Intelligence, led the function. The report records that she was expected to join OCBC as Head of the Group Data Office in March 2026, with a successor to be named. (Annual Report 2025, pp. 39 and 65)

Where the report pairs AI with governance, the mechanisms it cites are a Quality Assurance Scorecard, introduced to strengthen accountability and service quality, and the Cyber Trust Mark. (Annual Report 2025, p. 12)

What the annual report does not cover

An AI policy or principles, an inventory of AI systems with risk tiers, and model risk management for AI are not covered in the annual report. This describes public disclosure only, not an assessment of practice; such frameworks may exist internally or at OCBC group level, which we did not review.

How we searched

We searched the full text of all 1–244 pages of the Annual Report 2025 (counted as pages of the PDF file) for 17 terms that usually accompany a description of how AI itself is governed. Matching ignored capitalisation, except for the acronyms.

responsible AI · AI governance · AI risk · artificial intelligence risk · model risk · model validation · AI policy / principles / framework · AI ethics · FEAT · Veritas · fairness · explainability · human oversight / human-in-the-loop · algorithm · ISO/IEC 42001 · NIST AI RMF · MAS AI guidelines (AIRM)

Result. None of the 17 terms appears anywhere in the report.

What this search cannot show

  • Only the annual report was searched. The sustainability report, the company website, results materials and regulatory filings were not.
  • The search looks for exact terms. A framework described in other words would not be found.
  • Frameworks set by OCBC at group level may apply to Great Eastern; they were not reviewed.
  • Text taken from a PDF can split a phrase across columns, so a term could be missed. The governance and risk sections have not yet been read in full by a second reviewer.

Appendix B

Method

This report is built so that every statement can be traced to the page it comes from. The steps below apply to every quotation and every summary sentence in it.

1

Statements start from a stored copy of the source

Each statement is drawn from a document we hold a stored copy of. For this report that is Great Eastern’s Annual Report 2025, as published in March 2026. We record the page on which each statement appears.

2

Quotations are matched word for word

Every quotation is compared with the text of the stored copy, on the page cited, and is printed only if it matches word for word. Where a quotation is shortened, an ellipsis marks the gap and each remaining part is matched in order.

3

Status uses the company’s own wording

Where we say how far a use of AI has progressed, we use the company’s verb, such as “deployed”, “testing” or “developed”. A stated aim is never presented as a result.

4

Figures from tables are read from the stored copy

Figures taken from tables, such as the assumption sensitivities in section 4, are read from the stored copy each time the report is produced and checked against the values we expect; any total is recalculated from them.

5

Our sentences are kept apart from the company’s

Summary sentences, observations and hypotheses are ours. Each cites the pages it rests on and is checked against those pages for dropped qualifiers, such as “aims to”, before release.

6

Absence is reported as a search, not a finding

Where we say the report does not describe something, we state what we searched for, where, and what the search cannot show (section 5).

7

Later material is kept separate

Material we have located but not yet checked against a stored copy appears only in section 6, phrased as reported by its source and marked to be confirmed.

8

Independent review before release

Before a report is final, its findings are reviewed by an advisor who did not produce them. This discussion draft has not yet completed that review.
