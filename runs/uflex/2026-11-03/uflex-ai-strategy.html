1Summary

UFlex is in a growth phase. Egypt aseptic is in commercial trials, a 54,000-tonne BOPP line is coming at Dharwad, and Mexico WPP and the Noida recycling plant came on stream in 2026. Management's stated focus is "Focused investments in aseptic, WPP and recycling to accelerate growth". Film lines ran at 85.4% utilisation in Q1 FY27, with an EBITDA margin of 17.0% reported and 15.5% normalised.

AI can help in four places in this phase. It can bring new capacity to target output sooner, and take breaks, scrap and energy out of every kilogram on existing lines. It can lift recycling yields and produce the recycled-content records brand owners and regulators ask for. And it can give the enterprise better cash, inventory and FX forecasts across nine countries.

We recommend a staged programme. Stage 1 commits about ₹18 cr: one pilot film line chosen by UFlex, inventory planning, recycling quality at Noida and Dharwad instrumentation. The larger roll-out follows only if the pilot line, measured for six months against a control line, clears its own break-even.

What we recommend

  1. Start from what UFlex already runs. List the line-supplier digital services, inspection systems, Ceruflex remote monitoring and AI segregation already in place, so nothing is bought twice.
  2. Prove the film-line case on one line and one problem UFlex chooses. Measure for six months against a control line, using a design agreed with group finance. Roll out only if the line clears its own break-even.
  3. Run three enterprise-level use cases alongside. These are inventory planning, recycling quality and records at Noida, and Dharwad instrumentation from commissioning. None depends on the pilot line.
  4. Build the data foundation once, with records from resin to finished pack, because every use case and every recycled-content claim draws on it.
  5. Keep group finance as owner of the value ledger, so results are measured by the people who report them.

What makes UFlex distinctive

  1. It spans the chain from resin to pack. PET chips, film, metallising and coating, inks and adhesives, cylinders, converting and recycling sit inside one group, so the records behind a recycled-content or recyclability claim can be assembled in one place.
  2. It is adding capacity now. New lines are the cheapest place to build in data capture, during commissioning rather than as a retrofit.
  3. A film playbook can be repeated across nine countries. A result proven on one line can be replicated, which is where AI economics improve.
  4. It builds machines. Plant learning can become product features, and machines sold into the EU must meet the Machinery Regulation from January 2027.
  5. Its recycling capacity is commissioned and ramping, at Noida, Mexico, Egypt, Poland, Jammu and Malanpur.

3The industry

Film prices follow capacity. Listed peers describe raw materials as largely passed through and expect BOPET and BOPP margins to stay volatile as new lines arrive in India and China. The 2026 rise in film prices followed the West Asia conflict, and UFlex expects some normalisation. Through the cycle, a producer controls cost per kilogram, first-time-right production and mix.

Feedstock is more volatile. Indian PTA rose about 31% within days in March 2026 and polypropylene list prices rose by a cumulative ₹23 a kilogram, so planning and buying decisions carry more value than before.

Recycled content is becoming a legal and commercial requirement. India's Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2026 set recycled-content targets for producers, importers and brand owners. Flexible packaging rises from 10% to 20% by 2027-28, and multilayer from 5% to 10%. Exemptions apply where food-contact rules restrict recycled content, with a carry-forward for shortfalls. QR or barcode labelling has applied since July 2025. The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation has applied since 12 August 2026, with recyclability grades and recycled-content targets binding from 2030, averaged per plant per year. Brand owners will ask suppliers for records by plant and format.

5The strategy

Ambition. Use AI to bring new capacity to target output sooner, lower the cost of every kilogram on existing lines, make recycled-content records reliable, and improve cash and inventory decisions across nine countries, committing money in stages as results are measured.

Five strategic choices

ChoiceWhat it meansWhy, for UFlex
Build AI into new capacityInstrument Dharwad from commissioning; support the Egypt aseptic ramp-up.New lines are the cheapest place to capture data; each month to target output carries EBITDA.
Take loss out of every kilogramBreaks, gels, restart and changeover scrap, trim, energy per good kilogram.Cost per kilogram is what UFlex controls through the cycle.
Make recycled-content records reliableRecycling quality and records from bale to pack, aligned with recognised chain-of-custody models.PWM 2026, QR labelling and EU PPWR require it; brand owners will ask suppliers.
Improve enterprise decisionsInventory, procurement, cash and FX forecasting across nine currencies.About 100 days of inventory; FX losses in Nigeria, Egypt and Mexico drove exceptional items in FY24–FY25.
Commit in stagesOne line, six months, a control line; roll out only on measured results.Keeps the larger spend uncommitted until UFlex's own rates are known.

Guiding principles

  • Start from what is installed. Use OEM and inspection-vendor data feeds already paid for; add only what is missing.
  • Start with the data model. One model of site, line, grade, order and roll serves every use case and every recycled-content record.
  • Advise first. Thickness control and defect classification are usually automated on modern lines; AI adds cause-finding and early warning, and earns any closed-loop role only after a performance record.
  • Read-only from the plant floor. No remote access to OT networks; data is exported by UFlex IT.
  • Measure everything. Each use case has a baseline, a control where possible, an attribution rule signed by group finance, and a stop condition.

6Use cases

Six use cases form the programme. UC1, UC2, UC3 and UC6 produce recurring benefit; UC4 and UC5 produce one-off value that we report separately. Further use cases are sized in the sprint.

UC1 · Recurring · Stage 1 pilot

Film-line stability and yield

The problem
On modern lines, thickness control and defect classification are usually automated; on older lines profile-loop tuning may still pay. The larger losses are breaks at the MDO and TDO, gels and black specks from degraded polymer (dead spots, die build-up), restart scrap after breaks, changeover scrap and winding defects. Chip moisture and IV, drying and crystallisation, and the share of reclaim and rPET in the blend all feed these, and the rising recycled-content push makes blend control matter more.
What AI adds
Links each break or defect to its upstream cause across time lags and roll positions, from chip properties through casting and pinning, stretching and filter-change cycles to the winder; warns shift leads before a break; recommends blend, changeover and filter-change settings that cut restart and transition scrap; tracks energy per good kilogram and the carbon it represents.
Data needed
Chip lab data (IV, moisture), dryer and extruder tags, casting and pinning, MDO/TDO tags including clip and chain condition, break logs, scanner events, roll and grade records, energy meters. Most already exists in OEM, inspection and lab systems.
KPIs and baseline
Breaks per 1,000 running hours; scrap and off-spec kg per tonne; restart and changeover loss; kWh per good kg. A six-month historian pre-period and a matched control line; sample size set from historian variance in the sprint.
Value logic (base)
Film production 492,779 t × 0.6% avoidable scrap removed × ₹110/kg × 30% value lost after reclaim or sale as seconds, plus 2% energy per good kg on the film-line share of power and fuel = ₹17.9 cr a year at full coverage (range ₹7.9 cr to ₹32.0 cr). Throughput from fewer breaks is shown only as a memo (₹3.7 cr a year when lines are sold out).
Indicative cost
₹3 crore for the pilot line; about ₹1.5 crore a further site, varying by OEM and line vintage.
Evidence it works
Kunlene (BOPP films) defects −31%; Mondi Hilm rework and waste −50% (product not stated).
Main risks
Data held in OEM systems; operator trust; integration effort across OEMs and vintages.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Film technical and operations leadership, with the pilot plant head
UC2 · Recurring · After Gate 2

Slitting, deckle and line scheduling

The problem
Most producers already run deckle optimisation. The remaining gains are in sequencing grades and thicknesses on each line, in master-roll ageing constraints for BOPP before slitting, and in linking slitting plans to the order book and line schedule.
What AI adds
Extends existing trim optimisation with grade and thickness campaigning, run-rate and changeover-loss prediction, and master-roll ageing constraints.
Data needed
Order book, slitter set-ups, master-roll stock and age, changeover history, line run rates.
KPIs and baseline
Trim loss %; changeover time; on-time-in-full; finished-goods days (campaigning can raise stock, so this is tracked with UC5).
Value logic (base)
Film production × 0.2% trim removed beyond today's optimisation × ₹110/kg × 30% value lost = ₹3.3 cr a year at full coverage (range ₹1.3 cr to ₹7.9 cr). Changeover scrap is counted in UC1, not here.
Indicative cost
₹3.5 crore after Gate 2.
Evidence it works
Klöckner Pentaplast reports −3% waste from trim optimisation (vendor case study).
Main risks
Gains may be small where optimisation is already in place; planner adoption.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Planning and slitting leads at the film sites
UC3 · Recurring · Stage 1

Recycling quality and recycled-content records

The problem
Feedstock dominates rPET cost and quality. Bale quality, PVC and other contamination, colour and IV decide yield and whether output reaches food-contact grades through SSP decontamination and the relevant approvals. rMLP is lower-value. Recycled-content claims also need records that fit a recognised chain-of-custody model, such as physical segregation or mass balance (ISO 22095), and registered-recycler certificates under PWM.
What AI adds
Grades bales at purchase from images and lab samples; predicts yield and grade from feedstock and line data; flags contamination; keeps records from bale to resin to film to pack that fit the chosen chain-of-custody model. Record-keeping is data engineering; the AI is in grading and quality prediction.
Data needed
Bale data and images, washing and extrusion line data, SSP and lab tests (IV, colour, acetaldehyde), production genealogy across chip, film and converting, EPR filings.
KPIs and baseline
rPET yield per tonne of bales; share of output reaching food-contact grade; contamination rejects; share of output with complete records.
Value logic (base)
rPET capacity 79,020 t × utilisation (35% rising to 75%) × 2% yield and grade gain × ₹100/kg = ₹11.9 cr a year at 75% utilisation (range ₹4.6 cr to ₹22.2 cr), net of what existing AI segregation already delivers. The commercial value of records for customers is not counted.
Indicative cost
₹3.5 crore over three years.
Evidence it works
UFlex already reports AI-enabled segregation; HUL's Tinsukia Lighthouse (January 2025) cut sustainable-packaging trial time by 84%.
Main risks
Evidence standards still settling (EU methodology act expected end-2026); food-contact approvals; feedstock market.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Sustainability leadership, with the recycling plant heads
UC4 · One-off · Stage 1

New-capacity ramp-up

The problem
Dharwad BOPP is due in FY27–28 and can be instrumented from commissioning. Egypt aseptic is already in commercial trials, with first-year utilisation expected around 30%, so support there is a retrofit. Ramps are also limited by demand, which AI does not change.
What AI adds
Captures start-up, grade-qualification and first-time-right data from day one at Dharwad; applies UC1's cause models to ramp-up losses; supports laminate-quality learning in Egypt.
Data needed
Commissioning and line data, qualification trials, quality records.
KPIs and baseline
Months to target utilisation; first-pass yield on new grades; qualification trials per grade.
Value logic (base)
Half of management's guided ₹500 crore EBITDA (the Dharwad and Egypt share) ÷ 12 × 1 month earlier × 40% attributed to AI = ₹8.3 cr one-off, spread over the ramp (range ₹3.1 cr to ₹20.8 cr).
Indicative cost
₹2 crore.
Evidence it works
New lines are the cheapest place to build in data capture; no published ramp-up benchmark was found.
Main risks
Commissioning priorities; OEM data access during warranty; demand.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Project leadership for Dharwad BOPP and Egypt aseptic
UC5 · One-off cash · Stage 1

Planning, procurement and inventory

The problem
Inventories were ₹2,408 crore at 31 March 2026, about 100 days of cost of goods sold across nine countries, while PTA, MEG and polypropylene swing sharply. Lamination cure time and grade campaigning also hold stock.
What AI adds
Forecasts demand by grade, market and customer; sets inventory norms by item and plant; gives buyers price-risk signals; flags slow-moving stock and cure-time bottlenecks.
Data needed
ERP sales, stock, purchases and order book; production plans; supplier lead times; public polymer price series.
KPIs and baseline
Inventory days (not crore, since inventory grows with revenue); forecast accuracy; on-time-in-full.
Value logic (base)
5 days released × 50% attributed to AI = ₹61 cr one-off cash (range ₹36 cr to ₹97 cr). Interest saved is not counted as benefit. In a supply disruption UFlex may choose to hold more stock; this lever is a choice, not a forecast.
Indicative cost
₹3.5 crore build; a planning-engine licence from year 2.
Evidence it works
Multi-Color (labels) reports forecast accuracy up 50% (vendor case study).
Main risks
ERP data quality across countries; parallel spreadsheets.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Group finance with supply-chain leadership
UC6 · Recurring · After Gate 2

Gravure make-ready and colour

The problem
Register control and 100% print inspection are usually closed-loop already. The remaining losses are make-ready and colour approval, ink viscosity drift, reuse of returned inks, solvent use and recovery, and adhesive coat weight and cure in lamination.
What AI adds
Recommends press and ink settings from job history, cylinder engraving data and inline spectral colour measurement; predicts viscosity drift; supports colour formulation and returned-ink reuse in UFlex's own ink kitchen.
Data needed
Press data, cylinder engraving records, spectral colour data, ink formulation and viscosity, solvent and lamination data, job tickets.
KPIs and baseline
Make-ready waste metres per job; colour approval at first pull; solvent use per job; lamination rework.
Value logic (base)
Flexible-packaging output (about 78,700 t a year) × 0.4% waste removed × ₹170/kg × 80% value lost = ₹4.3 cr a year (range ₹2.1 cr to ₹7.5 cr).
Indicative cost
₹2.5 crore after Gate 2.
Evidence it works
Gravure 100% inspection at PPL cut print-quality complaints 50% (vendor case study).
Main risks
Short runs and many job types.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Flexible packaging operations leadership (Noida, Jammu)

9Value and ROI

Costs include 25% contingency, internal plant time, licences, sensors, travel and FX, and they vary by scenario: the low case assumes a 25% overrun. What moves the answer

A top-down check

At year 5 the base case's recurring benefit equals about 0.35% of FY26 materials cost plus power and fuel (₹34 cr against ₹9,616 crore). That is modest against the whole-site results reported by Lighthouse factories, which is consistent with a conservative case that leaves maintenance, metallising, chip plants, aseptic and enterprise AI unsized.

Governance

  • AI policy and inventory, aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, recording each system's purpose, data, owner, risk tier and human-oversight rule.
  • Human oversight: advisory by default; closed-loop control only after a documented performance record, with operator override.
  • OT security (IEC 62443): read-only exports, no remote vendor access without UFlex control, segmentation before any model connects.
  • Cross-border data: data flows and vendor licensing reviewed country by country with UFlex counsel.
  • Change: operator training and communication in local languages at each site, with plant champions.
