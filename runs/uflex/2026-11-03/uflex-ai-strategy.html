1Summary

UFlex is in a growth phase. It is commissioning Egypt aseptic and building a 54,000-tonne BOPP line at Dharwad; Mexico WPP and the Noida recycling plant came on stream in 2026; and its stated focus is "Focused investments in aseptic, WPP and recycling to accelerate growth". Film lines ran at 85.4% utilisation in Q1 FY27, with an EBITDA margin of 17.0%, though management expects some normalisation as prices settle.

AI can help in three ways in this phase: bring new capacity to target output faster and right first time; take scrap, breaks and energy out of every kilogram on existing lines; and turn UFlex's unusual control of the chain, from PET chips to film to converting to recycling, into recycled-content evidence that brand owners and regulators can check.

We recommend six use cases, proved on one line that UFlex chooses before anything scales, and one data foundation built once. The case is deliberately conservative, and on those conservative rates a nine-country programme does not yet pay for itself. That is the point of the pilot: it measures UFlex's real rates against a clear break-even line before the larger spend is committed.

What we recommend

  1. Start by listing what UFlex already runs. Line suppliers' digital services, installed inspection systems, Ceruflex remote monitoring and the AI segregation in recycling, so nothing is paid for twice.
  2. Prove value on one line and one problem that UFlex chooses. For example, film breaks on a BOPP line or restart scrap on a BOPET line, measured for six months against a control line.
  3. Run three enterprise-level use cases in parallel. These are recycling quality and recycled-content evidence at Noida, instrumentation of the Dharwad and Egypt ramp-ups, and planning and inventory. None depends on the line pilot.
  4. Scale only on evidence. Scale the film-line work to other sites only if the pilot clears the break-even line. Overseas sites follow India, and Russia waits for a sanctions and data-transfer review.
  5. Build the data foundation once, with genealogy from resin to finished pack. It serves every use case and the recycled-content claims customers will ask for.

What makes UFlex distinctive

  1. It controls the chain from resin to pack. PET chips, film, metallising and coating, inks and adhesives, cylinders, converting and recycling sit under one roof. So the evidence for a recycled-content or recyclability claim can be assembled inside one company, which few competitors can offer Nestlé, PepsiCo or Mondelez.
  2. It is adding capacity now. New lines are the cheapest place to build in data and AI, because instrumentation goes in during commissioning, not as a retrofit.
  3. A film playbook can be repeated across nine countries. A result proven on one line can be replicated, which is where AI economics improve.
  4. It builds machines. Plant learning can become product features, and machine sales into the EU will need to meet the Machinery Regulation from January 2027.
  5. Its recycling capacity is built and running. Noida was commissioned in 2026, alongside sites in Mexico, Egypt, Poland, Jammu and Malanpur.

3The industry

Film prices follow capacity. Peers describe raw materials as largely passed through, with prices set by supply and demand, and expect volatile BOPET and BOPP margins as new lines arrive in India and China. The 2026 spike in film prices followed the West Asia conflict; UFlex itself expects some normalisation. Cost per kilogram and first-time-right production are what a producer controls through the cycle.

Feedstock is more volatile. Indian PTA rose about 31% within days in March 2026 and polypropylene list prices rose by a cumulative ₹23 a kilogram, so planning and buying decisions carry more value than before.

Recycled content is becoming a legal and commercial requirement. Under India's Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2026, flexible packaging must reach 10% recycled content, rising to 20% by 2027-28, and multilayer packaging 5%, rising to 10%. QR or barcode labelling has applied since July 2025, and shortfalls attract ₹5,000 a tonne in environmental compensation. The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation has applied since 12 August 2026, with recyclability grades and recycled-content targets binding from 2030, averaged per plant per year. Both require evidence by plant and format.

5The strategy

Ambition. Use AI to bring UFlex's new capacity to target output faster and right first time, to lower the cost of every kilogram on existing lines, and to make every recycled-content and recyclability claim provable, proving each step in rupees before it scales.

Four strategic choices

ChoiceWhat it meansWhy, for UFlex
Build AI into new capacityInstrument Dharwad BOPP and Egypt aseptic from commissioning; first-time-right grades and faster ramp-up.Management guides about ₹500 crore of EBITDA from four projects at decent utilisation; each month earlier matters.
Make compliance evidence a commercial assetGenealogy from resin to pack and recycling quality data, offered to brand owners.PWM 2026, QR labelling and EU PPWR require it, and UFlex controls more of the chain than its peers.
Take loss out of every kilogramBreaks, gels, restart and changeover scrap, trim and energy per good kilogram.Film prices follow capacity; cost per kilogram is what UFlex controls through the cycle.
Prove once, then replicateOne line, one problem, six months, a control line; then a packaged playbook for other sites.Replication across nine countries is where the economics improve, if the first result is real.

Guiding principles

  • Start from what is installed. Use OEM and inspection-vendor data feeds already paid for; add only what is missing.
  • Start with the data model. One model of site, line, grade, order and roll serves every use case and every compliance claim.
  • Advise first, automate later. Thickness control and defect classification are already automated on modern lines; AI adds cause-finding and advice before it earns any closed-loop role.
  • Data stays on UFlex-controlled infrastructure. Cross-border transfers are reviewed country by country; the Russia site is excluded until a sanctions and data-transfer review clears it.
  • Measure everything. Each use case has a baseline, a control where possible, a finance-signed attribution rule and a stop condition.

6Use cases

Six use cases form the programme. Four produce recurring EBITDA and carry the business case; two produce one-off value that we report separately. Five more are wave 2, sized in the sprint once UFlex's data is available.

UC1 · Recurring · Pilot

Film-line stability and yield

The problem
On modern film lines, thickness control and defect classification are already automated. The loss sits elsewhere: film breaks at the MDO and TDO, gels and black specks from filtration and melt-pump pressure, restart scrap after breaks, changeover scrap, and winding defects such as gauge bands, wrinkles and telescoping.
What AI adds
Links each break or defect to its upstream cause across time lags and roll positions; warns shift leads before a break; recommends changeover sequences and settings that cut restart and transition scrap; tracks energy per good kilogram.
Data needed
Line control tags at 1-second resolution, scanner defect events, break logs, filtration and melt-pump data, roll and grade records, energy meters. Most of this already exists in OEM and inspection systems.
KPIs and baseline
Breaks per 1,000 running hours; scrap and off-spec kg per tonne; restart and changeover loss; kWh per good kg. Baseline and control line agreed with finance before go-live.
Value logic (base)
Film production 492,779 t × 0.6% avoidable scrap removed × ₹110/kg × 30% value lost after reclaim or sale as seconds, plus 2% energy per good kg on the film-line share of power and fuel = ₹17.9 cr a year at full scale (range ₹7.9 cr to ₹32.0 cr).
Indicative cost
₹3.5 crore at the pilot site; about ₹1.5 crore a further site, varying by OEM and line vintage.
Evidence it works
Kunlene (BOPP) defects −31% and ACG defects −71% (WEF Lighthouses); Mondi Hilm rework and waste −50%.
Main risks
Data locked in OEM systems; operator trust; lines from different OEMs and vintages need separate integration.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Head of technical and film operations, with the pilot plant's head
UC2 · Recurring · After the pilot

Slitting, deckle and line scheduling

The problem
Turning master rolls into slit rolls, planning trim across orders, and sequencing grades and thicknesses on each line decide much of the yield outside the line itself.
What AI adds
Optimises deckle and slitting plans across open orders, and campaigns grades and thicknesses to cut changeover scrap and time, with machine learning to predict run rates and changeover losses.
Data needed
Order book, slitter configurations, roll inventory, changeover history, line run rates.
KPIs and baseline
Trim loss %; changeover scrap and time; on-time-in-full.
Value logic (base)
Film production × 0.2% trim and changeover scrap removed × ₹110/kg × 30% value lost = ₹3.3 cr a year at full scale (range ₹1.3 cr to ₹7.9 cr). On-time delivery gains are not counted.
Indicative cost
₹5 crore over three years.
Evidence it works
Klöckner Pentaplast reports −3% waste from trim optimisation (vendor case study).
Main risks
Planner adoption; order-book data quality.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Heads of planning and slitting at the film sites
UC3 · Recurring · Wave 1

Recycling quality and recycled-content evidence

The problem
Recycled-content targets now bind under PWM 2026 and, for EU customers, PPWR from 2030. Brand owners need evidence by plant and format. Recycling lines depend on feedstock quality: contamination, colour and IV in rPET; mixed layers in rMLP.
What AI adds
AI sorting and quality prediction on recycling lines (IV, colour, contamination). Mass-balance and genealogy records from bale to resin to film to pack, packaged as customer-ready evidence of recycled content and recyclability.
Data needed
Bale and feedstock records, line sensors and lab tests on recycling lines, production genealogy across chip, film and converting, EPR filings.
KPIs and baseline
Yield and grade mix on rPET and rMLP; contamination rejects; share of output with complete evidence; customer evidence requests met.
Value logic (base)
Management's recycling revenue potential of ₹850 crore × 1.5% yield and grade gain = ₹12.8 cr a year at the realised share of potential. The commercial value of evidence for customers is not counted.
Indicative cost
₹5 crore over three years.
Evidence it works
UFlex already reports AI-enabled segregation systems; HUL's Tinsukia Lighthouse cut sustainable-packaging trial time by 84%.
Main risks
Evidence standards still settling (PPWR methodology act expected end-2026); food-contact rules.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Head of sustainability, with the recycling plant heads
UC4 · One-off · Wave 1

New-capacity ramp-up and first-time-right

The problem
Egypt aseptic is in commercial trials with first-year utilisation expected around 30%; Dharwad BOPP is due in FY27–28. Every month to target output carries EBITDA.
What AI adds
Instruments the new lines from commissioning, so start-up, grade qualification and first-time-right data are captured from day one; applies UC1 cause models to ramp-up losses.
Data needed
Commissioning and line data, qualification trials, quality records.
KPIs and baseline
Months to target utilisation; first-pass yield on new grades; qualification trials per grade.
Value logic (base)
Guided EBITDA of about ₹500 crore a year from four projects ÷ 12 × 1 month earlier × 50% attributed to AI = ₹20.8 cr one-off (range ₹10.4 cr to ₹41.7 cr).
Indicative cost
₹3 crore.
Evidence it works
New lines are the cheapest place to build in instrumentation; no published ramp-up benchmark was found.
Main risks
Commissioning teams' priorities; OEM data access during warranty.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Project heads for Dharwad BOPP and Egypt aseptic
UC5 · One-off cash · Wave 1

Planning, procurement and inventory

The problem
Inventories were ₹2,408 crore at 31 March 2026, about 100 days of cost of goods sold across nine countries, while PTA, MEG and polypropylene swing sharply. Lamination cure time also holds stock.
What AI adds
Forecasts demand by grade, market and customer; sets inventory norms by item and plant; gives buyers price-risk signals; flags slow-moving stock and cure-time bottlenecks.
Data needed
ERP sales, stock, purchases and order book; production plans; supplier lead times; public polymer price series.
KPIs and baseline
Inventory days by category and plant; forecast accuracy; on-time-in-full.
Value logic (base)
5 days of inventory released × 50% attributed to AI = ₹61 cr one-off cash (range ₹36 cr to ₹97 cr). Interest saved is not counted as benefit. In a supply disruption UFlex may choose to hold more stock; this lever is a choice, not a forecast.
Indicative cost
₹5 crore build plus ₹2 crore a year planning-engine licence.
Evidence it works
Multi-Color (labels) reports forecast accuracy up 50% (vendor case study); no credible public figure for packaging working capital.
Main risks
ERP data quality across countries; parallel spreadsheets.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Group finance with the head of supply chain
UC6 · Recurring · Wave 2

Gravure make-ready and colour

The problem
Make-ready and colour-matching waste on gravure presses, ink viscosity drift and solvent use in the Noida and Jammu converting plants. Artwork checking is a software purchase, not part of this strategy.
What AI adds
Recommends press and ink settings from job history, cylinder engraving data and colour measurement; predicts viscosity drift; supports colour formulation in UFlex's own ink kitchen.
Data needed
Press data, cylinder engraving records, colour measurements, ink formulation and viscosity data, job tickets.
KPIs and baseline
Make-ready waste metres per job; colour approval at first pull; solvent use per job.
Value logic (base)
Packaging volume 151,755 t × 0.4% waste removed × ₹170/kg × 80% value lost = ₹8.3 cr a year at full scale (range ₹4.1 cr to ₹14.4 cr).
Indicative cost
₹3 crore.
Evidence it works
Published converter waste: printing 3–5%, lamination 1–2%. Gravure 100% inspection at PPL cut print complaints 50% (vendor case study).
Main risks
Short runs and many job types.
Natural owner (to be agreed)
Head of flexible packaging operations (Noida, Jammu)

8Value and ROI

Method. Three kinds of value are kept apart. Recurring EBITDA from the plant and recycling use cases, net of the programme's own running cost, is the headline. The one-off EBITDA from a faster ramp-up is reported separately. So is the working-capital release, of which we attribute half to AI; interest on it is never counted as benefit. Plant value is built on tonnes, not revenue shares. Costs include 25% contingency, internal plant time, licences, sensors, travel and FX, and they vary by scenario (the low case assumes a 25% overrun). The decision rule. On our conservative base rates, the full nine-country programme does not pay for itself within five years (NPV −₹47 cr on EBITDA; about ₹4 cr once the inventory release is included). It breaks even if UFlex's measured rates are about 1.6 times our base assumptions, which means removing about 0.95% of film production as scrap and saving about 3.2% of energy per good kilogram. Those rates are well below what the Kunlene and ACG Lighthouses report, but they are not proven at UFlex. The pilot exists to measure them. Below the line, UFlex keeps the enterprise use cases and the data foundation, and does not scale the film-line work.

Assumptions to replace with UFlex data first

  1. Avoidable scrap, breaks and restart loss by line and grade, and the value recovered through reclaim and seconds.
  2. Energy per kilogram by line and grade, and the film-line share of power and fuel.
  3. Recycling line yields and grade mix; evidence requests from customers.
  4. Inventory by category, plant and age; current forecast accuracy.
  5. Vendor, OEM and partner quotes for the build, by line type and vintage.

The readiness sprint (two weeks)

Days 1–3 Immerse: a walk-through of the line UFlex nominates, interviews, and an inventory of the AI, automation and line-supplier digital services already installed. Days 4–7 Audit: data availability and quality for the chosen problem; the IT and OT landscape; the security protocol. Days 8–11 Prioritise: rebuild the model on UFlex numbers and set the break-even line with finance. Days 12–14 Decide: a working session on the pilot scope, the control line and the measurement plan.

Governance

  • AI policy and inventory, aligned with ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, recording each system's purpose, data, owner, risk tier and human-oversight rule.

12Next step

We propose a two-week readiness sprint on one line and one problem that UFlex nominates, preceded by a 45-minute working session with film technical leadership and group finance to choose them.

Commercial terms we would propose

  • Sprint: a fixed fee in the range of ₹15–25 lakh. UFlex keeps every deliverable, with no exclusivity and no obligation to continue.
  • Pilot: advisory fees capped, with 30–50% at risk against the finance-signed baseline on the control line.
  • Team: a lead advisor and a film-process specialist with BOPET and BOPP line experience, named with references in the proposal.
  • Independence: no fees from historian, vision or planning vendors; a written conflict-of-interest declaration; NDA and data-security terms covering CERT-In and DPDP.

What we would need from UFlex

  • Access to the chosen line, its engineers, and its control, inspection and energy data
  • The list of installed line-supplier digital services and inspection systems
  • ERP extracts for inventory, sales and purchases (24 months)
  • Recycling line data and customer evidence requests
  • Interviews with 6–8 leaders across films, recycling, packaging, finance, supply chain and IT
