# UFlex Limited: Outside-in Industry Research for the AI Strategy

**Prepared by:** PLUMB Advisory, industry research workstream
**Research date:** 3 Nov 2026
**Method:** Public sources only, accessed through web search and fetch on 3 Nov 2026. Every figure below is quoted from its source with the publication date where the source gives one. Where a source gives no date, the table says "n.d.". Where no reliable public figure was found, the text says **NOT FOUND**. Nothing has been estimated or filled in from memory.

**Evidence labels for AI outcomes**
- **CUSTOMER-REPORTED**: the manufacturer itself, or an independent body that assessed it (for example, a WEF Lighthouse citation), states the result.
- **VENDOR CASE STUDY**: the technology vendor's marketing states the result. It is not independently verified.
- **ANALYST/SURVEY**: a consultancy, survey or academic aggregate.

**Scope cautions.** The figures for "flexible packaging" depend heavily on how each publisher defines the market. Several of the AI results below come from adjacent sub-industries (pharma packaging materials, labels, blown film, paper) rather than from BOPET/BOPP film lines. Each table row states the exact scope.

## 0. Most decision-relevant findings for the UFlex AI strategy

1. **The core film business is cyclical, and the cycle is set by capacity, not cost.** Polyplex's Q4 FY26 deck says film "Pricing is a function of demand/supply - effect of crude is limited" and that raw materials are largely "pass through". Both Polyplex and Cosmo First (May 2026) expect margins to stay volatile while new lines arrive. Cosmo sees "seven to eight lines coming in India" over the next three years. For AI, this argues for use cases that pay off at the trough: yield and scrap, energy, grade-change, and maintenance. It argues against use cases whose value depends on price.
2. **2026 brought a feedstock shock tied to the West Asia conflict.** PTA in India rose "nearly 31 per cent" within days in March 2026. Indian PP list prices rose by a cumulative ₹23/kg by 11 March 2026. UFlex's own September 2026 deck says the conflict is "disrupting packaging film raw material production and transit routes". Procurement and price-forecasting analytics therefore matter more than they did in 2024.
3. **UFlex's own film-line utilisation has run at 77–85% since FY23.** Its Sept 2026 deck shows FY26 at 77.5% and Q1 FY27 at 85.4%. Throughput gains from AI are therefore worth less than cost-per-kg gains (scrap, energy, changeover) until utilisation tightens again.
4. **Regulation has now made traceability concrete.** India's PWM (Amendment) Rules 2026 (G.S.R. 237(E), 31 Mar 2026) set recycled-content targets: Category II flexible 10% → 20% and Category III multilayer 5% → 10% by 2027-28. India's QR/barcode labelling has been in force since 1 Jul 2025. The EU PPWR has applied since 12 Aug 2026, with recyclability grades binding from 2030 and recycled-content targets from 1 Jan 2030. Data on recycled-content and recyclability attributes is becoming a commercial requirement.
5. **There are two directly relevant WEF Lighthouses, and both are in Asia.** ACG Packaging Materials (Shirwal, India; Jan 2026) reports lead time −40%, raw material costs −20%, defects −71%, energy −31% and OTIF +34%. Kunlene Film Industries (Suzhou, China; packaging film, a BOPP producer per a 2018 Brückner release) reports defects −31%, R&D lead time −45% and minimum order size −83%. These are the most defensible peer benchmarks available.
6. **Indian listed peers disclose almost no AI.** Polyplex, Cosmo First and Huhtamaki India describe only "digital" or "digitalisation", with no quantified AI outcomes. SRF reports ML-assisted problem-solving and digital twins at two sites, but not in its packaging films business. Among global peers, only Amcor (10-K AI risk factor; CIO interview describing vision, predictive maintenance and changeover RCA) and Mondi (plant-level ML results) disclose specific AI work. A visible, measured programme would set UFlex apart in India.
