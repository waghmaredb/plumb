# UFlex test run — red-team findings and product lessons (2026-11-03)

v1 reviewed by two independent executive personas (CEO + head of film technology; CFO). v2 rewrote the strategy.

## What changed v1 → v2
- Thesis: "win the trough on cost per kg" → growth phase (new capacity ramp-up, recycled-content evidence, cost per kg). Q1 FY27 figures used (85.4% utilisation, 17.0% margin).
- Use cases rebuilt around how film lines actually lose money (breaks, gels, restart/changeover scrap, winding), plus slitting/deckle/scheduling, recycling quality and evidence in wave 1, new-capacity ramp-up; metallisers, coating, chip-plant control, aseptic moved to a sized-in-sprint wave 2. Artwork checking = buy.
- ROI: three value streams separated (recurring EBITDA net of programme cost; one-off ramp-up; working capital with 50% attribution; interest memo only). Five years. Tonnage-based. Scope-matched bases. Costs rebuilt for nine countries with 25% contingency, internal time, licences; scenario-varying. Russia excluded pending sanctions review.
- Result: base case does not pay at conservative rates (5-yr NPV −₹47 cr on EBITDA); break-even at ~1.6× base rates (≈0.95% scrap removed, 3.2% energy per good kg) — becomes the pilot decision rule.
- Removed: CRISIL/leverage framing, "Discover" maturity label, named executive sponsors, "illegible filing" line, Cloudster name in the workbook.
- Offer: sprint ₹15–25 lakh fixed; pilot fees capped with 30–50% at risk; six-month measurement with a control line; film-process specialist on the team; existing-tools inventory first.

## Lessons for the product (to encode)
1. Domain depth check: a use case must name the actual loss mechanisms of the client's process and acknowledge what is already automated (rubric rule).
2. Thesis must use the latest quarter and the client's own stated strategy, not the trough narrative.
3. Never mix stock/one-off and recurring value; interest on released cash is never "recurring".
4. Value bases must match the use case's scope; no revenue-share proxies for physical processes.
5. Costs must scale with sites and vary by scenario; include contingency, internal time, licences.
6. No credit-rating or leverage framing in client-facing material.
7. Roles, not names, for sponsors until agreed.
8. Start every plant strategy with an inventory of what the client already runs and pays for.
9. Sanctions/data-transfer review for multi-country footprints.
